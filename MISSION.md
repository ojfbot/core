# MISSION — shape-up

## The capability

**Diagnose a planning system with Shape Up as the lens, and decide what to adopt without
cargo-culting it.**

Given a piece of planning machinery — the fleet's roadmap, Control-Gated Slices, a client's
process — determine what it fixes, what it lets float, and what it does when work overruns; then
say whether a Shape Up mechanism is worth importing *on the evidence of a local problem*, not on
the evidence that Basecamp liked it.

Not a subject ("learn Shape Up"). Not coverage of 23 sections. One portable instrument, run cold.

## Where the model is likely wrong — the ZPD target

Hypothesis, from their own words in a prior record (*"I can't tell absorb from cargo-cult"*):

> That the decision to adopt a mechanism turns on how good the mechanism is. It turns on whether a
> problem exists locally, independent of the source. The circuit breaker is compelling *and* the
> fleet has a matching problem — which makes it easy to adopt for the wrong reason and never notice,
> because the outcome looks identical either way until it fails.

Second, narrower hypothesis: that "the fleet has no circuit breaker" means CGS lacks a stopping
rule of any kind. It has one — for *measurement breach* (stay in Brassboard, re-sample). What it
lacks is one for *time overrun*. Conflating those two is the likely error, and it is the difference
between a real gap and an imagined one.
